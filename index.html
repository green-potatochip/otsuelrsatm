ABSTRACT

Open UWB ranging, without surveying the anchors.

Surveying UWB anchors adds deployment effort when ranging infrastructure is installed. OUR-SLAM is a VIO-driven framework for jointly estimating robot poses, initially unknown anchor positions, and biases. Its initializer combines density-normalized geometric readiness with bias-constrained robust estimation and trajectory uncertainty to suppress large position–bias errors. After registration, asynchronous ranges are evaluated at acquisition time, while information-based admission with capped directional reweighting suppresses redundant factors. Experiments on corrupted Monte Carlo simulations, NTU VIRAL, and an in-house dataset demonstrate robust initialization and improved localization over VIO.

THE IDEA

Let the robot discover its anchors.

UWB ranges constrain visual-inertial drift, even when anchor positions have never been surveyed. OUR-SLAM estimates the trajectory, anchor positions, and range biases together.

01 / INITIALIZATION

Initialize with care

Challenge Limited viewing directions let anchor position errors hide inside a range bias.

Approach Balance repeated positions, test geometric readiness, and refine robustly with a bias constraint and trajectory uncertainty.

02 / RANGE ADMISSION

Select for information

Challenge High-rate ranges can add redundant factors without comparable new information.

Approach Evaluate ranges at acquisition time and use decayed information gain to select useful measurements.

03 / JOINT ESTIMATION

Refine together

Challenge Fixing an inaccurate anchor can carry its error into subsequent localization.

Approach Keep poses, anchor positions, and biases active in one incremental graph.

01 / ROBUST ANCHOR INITIALIZATION

A good fit needs good geometry.

Similar lines of sight let anchor displacement hide inside a range bias. Three stages turn an uncertain VIO path and raw ranges into an anchor position and bias that can enter the graph.

Geometry is a 2D illustration of the 3D initializer. The bias bound applies during initialization; registered anchors and biases remain free to refine in the backend.

02 / ACQUISITION-TIME RANGE FACTORS

Put the measurement at its own time.

A UWB range arrives between two odometry states. Interpolate translation and rotation, then apply the antenna lever arm. The measured distance stays unchanged, and one range factor connects the bracketing poses, anchor, and bias.

03 / INFORMATION-AWARE ADMISSION

Admit new information. Preserve aligned evidence.

After a residual gate, each candidate is tested against the decayed information matrix of its anchor–tag stream. Repeated directions bring less new information. Rejected candidates add no factors; their information can increase the weight of the next admitted factor when the directions align.

2 Reweight in the admitted direction

Project the rejected information in D onto qₖ to increase the next admitted factor’s weight.

γₖ = clip(1 + qₖᵀDqₖ / (qₖᵀqₖ)², 1, γ_max)

A 2D schematic of the full 4D anchor–bias information space, where q = [uᵀ, 1]ᵀ / σ. Rejected ranges add no graph factors. Aligned information increases one admitted factor’s weight up to γ_max. Clear D after this admission.

Admitted factors update H with γqqᵀ. Only candidates rejected by information admission contribute to D; ranges that fail the residual gate do not. Poses, anchors, and biases are jointly refined in iSAM2.
